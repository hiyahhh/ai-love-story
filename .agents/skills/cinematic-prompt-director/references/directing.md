# Directing rules

## Space and first frame

Describe physical relationships first: who sits/stands where, facing whom, separation needed for contact, depth layers, landmarks and camera side. Use approximate coordinates only when helpful. Maintain the action line for coverage; an intentional crossing needs a motivated transition or re-establishing shot.

For each segment specify its first visible composition. Establish important objects before use; stage later entrances explicitly. State the final pose/cut point needed by the next shot.

## Framing, optics and placement

Choose visible content and feasible placement before numbers. Tiny people in wide geography cannot carry subtle eye acting. Use body movement for a tableau and closer framing for micro-expression.

Use plain terms by default: natural portrait proportions, wide spatial context, near foreground exaggeration or distant compressed observation. Numeric FOV/focal length is optional. State diagonal/horizontal/vertical FOV and sensor/crop assumptions when giving equivalents. Focal length alone does not define a frame; avoid universal numeric safe zones or claims that a model obeys a lens number.

Camera distance determines perspective. Focal length/FOV determines framing from that position. Describe distance and subject size together. Specify height, side, distance, aim, roll, foreground anchor and travel path as relevant. A low angle needs a low viewpoint; an over-shoulder needs a near shoulder anchoring the far subject. Roll tilts the frame without changing camera side or mirroring geography.

Give movement a purpose: push for realization, tracking for connection to movement, locked framing for observation. Keep performance readable.

## Creative devices

Define trigger, start frame, physical/optical behavior, landing, hold and stable anchors. Usually choose one dominant device per short shot. A static composition can accompany one timed move.

- Crash zoom: one fast change from start framing to reaction framing, then hold. Do not also claim lens lock.
- Whip pan: trigger, fast pan with directional blur, readable landing and preserved world map.
- Dutch angle: roll direction/amount, held or settling, same physical camera side.
- Dolly zoom: keep subject size approximately constant while changing camera distance and focal length together. Moving closer requires a shorter focal length/wider FOV; moving away requires a longer focal length/narrower FOV. Do not claim a closer camera plus narrower FOV preserves subject size.
- Rack focus: define near/far planes and attention shift. Use separate cuts when focus control is unreliable.
- Match cut: match outgoing/incoming shape, composition or movement; execute precise timing in editing when necessary.
- Named influence: translate into observable framing, rhythm, light, texture and acting rather than stacking names.

## Acting and physics

State what the person wants, what changes and the small visible behavior carrying it. List only a few motivated reactions. Listeners respond to the event rather than constantly fidgeting. Preserve supplied dialogue and allow plausible speaking/listening time; flag lines that cannot fit instead of truncating them.

For action, ground anticipation, acceleration, contact, follow-through and settling. For intimate beats, specify important hands, object support and weight shifts without imposing an exhaustive physics checklist. Cloth/hair follow movement with natural delay.

## Light, motion and sound

Motivate light from window, practical, sky or another stated source and preserve direction across connected cuts. Reference sheets may use diffuse flat light even when the scene does not. Do not impose backlight, deep shadows or beauty fill universally.

Fast movement can have brief natural directional blur with readable holds. Avoid universal 'no blur' instructions. Shutter-angle language describes an intended look unless the target offers an actual setting. Prompt wording cannot establish that a playback/frame-rate fault is repaired; inspect the file.

Separate ambience, effects, dialogue, music and narration. Include only authorized categories and preserve exact speaker order. Plan external sound where needed.
